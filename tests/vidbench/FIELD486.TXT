VIDEO PLAYER - 486 MEASUREMENT DISK
===================================

A bootable fixed disk: the os8088 kernel, the Video
Player, three benches and some test clips (read
this file on the host: the disk has no text viewer).
What it measures becomes a new encoder target (a
"486" profile), the way the 286-vga one was made.

  17 sectors, 15 heads, 250 cylinders (31 MB), IDE.
  In 86Box: `make 486-video`, or your own 486 with
  this VHD on its IDE channel at that geometry and
  no floppy in A:, so it boots the disk. Put the
  blank floppy VID486OUT.IMG in B: - the results go
  on it.

PLEASE NOTE THE MACHINE: CPU and clock, the video
card and its BUS (ISA 8 or 16-bit, VLB, PCI), the
disk controller, the sound card. A VLB or PCI card is
a different machine to an ISA one at the same clock,
and both are worth a run if you have them.

CLOSE EACH BENCH WINDOW before the next one, and all
of them before playing a clip: each keeps its memory
until it is closed, and the player reads ahead into
whatever is free.

1. VIDBENCH.O88 -> VIDBENCH.TXT   (R)
     The card. Raw rows: 8,000 bytes to the screen
     and to RAM, READ from the screen, stored in mode
     12h, and the LATCH COPY (screen to screen, all
     four planes a byte) the flipped player takes.
     Then every frame of VIDBENCH.DAT decoded and
     timed. Screen garbage while it runs is expected.

2. VIDDISK.O88 -> VDWRITE.TXT, VIDDISK.TXT  (W, R)
     The disk. W writes its own stream file,
     STREAM.DAT, 12.5 MB - minutes, slower towards
     the end. R then reads it back (about two
     minutes), and the part that matters is the
     SILENT PLAYER'S CEILING: KB/s while an
     interrupt holds 0, 25, 50 and 75% of every
     frame. The screen stays still for 25 s of that;
     it is working. D deletes STREAM.DAT after.

3. VIDSND.O88 -> VIDSND.TXT       (R)
     The Sound Blaster: interrupts a frame, and the
     disk while the sound interrupt is busy.

4. THE CLIPS: double-click one. It opens in
     VIDEOD.O88, the Video Player with more lines on
     its info card; Space plays it. After each play
     press i and PHOTOGRAPH THE CARD: drawn, stalls,
     late, pauses, and the Heap / Ring / Lead / Dry
     lines. VIDEO.O88 is the ordinary player, the same
     without those lines.

     Last Exile, 0:48 to 0:58, lossless Mode X,
     24 fps, 22 kHz - its fastest pans and a scene
     change:
       LX32.V88    32 KB frames, no flip (TEARS)
       LX32F.V88   32 KB, flipped
       LX48F.V88   48 KB, flipped - no frame here is
                   cut at 48, so 63.5 would play the
                   same; it needs no clip of its own
       LX48B.V88   48 KB, no flip, in 12 bands
                   (tears along one line)
     The flipped 48 needs a player from 2026-10-06
     or later, and says so if not.
     Zero stalls and zero late is the target; the
     biggest frame cap that gets there is the 486's.

5. THE RESULTS: open the Disk window on B:, and copy
     VIDBENCH.TXT, VDWRITE.TXT, VIDDISK.TXT and
     VIDSND.TXT from C: onto it (drag them).
     Shut down, and the
     host can read VID486OUT.IMG as a FAT floppy
     (7-Zip, WinImage, or mount it).

Nothing on the disk is changed but the .TXT results
and VIDDISK's STREAM.DAT.
