VIDEO PLAYER - PICOMEM PROFILE BENCHES
======================================

The benches that turn a machine with a PicoMEM
into a video encoder profile (tools/os88venc.py):
"286-picomem1" for the Victory 286 with its
PicoMEM 1.x, "5150-picomem2" for the 5150 with a
2.x. This round REMODELS 286-picomem1 - the
profile the 286 is encoded for now that its disk
is the card's - and settles what LOADALL is worth
(docs/plans/LOADALL-PLAN.md).

  VIDBENCH.O88  how fast THIS CPU and card decode a
  VIDBENCH.DAT  frame (the DAT must sit beside it)
  VIDDISK.O88   how fast THIS disk streams a file,
                and extended memory (X, L)
  VIDSND.O88    whether the card's Sound Blaster
                gives one interrupt a frame
  VIDEOD.O88    the Video Player with the info
                card's diagnostic lines
  VIDEOR.O88    VIDEOD that also logs every read
                (item 8)
  README.TXT    this file

COPY ALL SEVEN INTO ONE EMPTY FOLDER ON THE
PICOMEM'S DISK (C:). VIDDISK measures the disk it
runs FROM. Each bench saves its report as a .TXT
beside itself, and saves NUMBER THEMSELVES - the
first VIDDISK report is VIDDISK1.TXT, the next
VIDDISK2.TXT - so passes never overwrite each
other. An EMPTY folder is what makes the numbers
below mean the setting: 1 = 14 wait states, 2 = 8.
The status line names the file each save went to.

PLEASE NOTE THE MACHINE: CPU and clock, video card
and bus, which drive letter is the PicoMEM's, the
PicoMEM firmware version, and the SD card.

TWO ROUNDS ON THE 286 - THE BUS WAIT STATES
---
The board copies 25% faster than it did on
2026-10-08 (0.257 ms a KB against 0.339), and the
bus going from 14 wait states to 8 ("Tune") is the
suspect. So every bench runs TWICE: round 1 at 14
wait states, round 2 at 8. On the 5150 there is
one round; skip X and L there (286 only).

HOW - close each window before opening the next
---
ROUND 1: set the bus to 14 WAIT STATES, boot.
1. VIDBENCH.O88, R. Screen garbage while it runs
   is the frames being drawn. -> VIDBENCH1.TXT
2. VIDDISK.O88, W. Writes STREAM.DAT, 12.5 MB, beside
   itself (minutes). -> VDWRITE1.TXT
   Then R (about a minute). -> VIDDISK1.TXT
   Then X (extended memory). -> VDXMS1.TXT
   Then L (LOADALL). -> VDLALL1.TXT
   Then E (the PicoMEM's EMS). -> VDEMS1.TXT
   LEAVE STREAM.DAT: round 2 reads it again.
3. VIDSND.O88, R (about a minute; expect silence
   - the count of interrupts is the answer).
   -> VIDSND1.TXT. It no longer crashes on the
   PicoMEM: it sets the card up the way SOUND.DRV
   does. If it still ends in STACK OVERFLOW or
   hangs, note it and go on.

ROUND 2: set the bus to 8 WAIT STATES, boot.
4. VIDBENCH R, VIDDISK R, X, L and E, VIDSND R -
   the same as round 1 but NOT W. Every file ends
   in 2: VIDBENCH2.TXT, VIDDISK2.TXT ...
   Then VIDDISK D deletes STREAM.DAT.

5. OPTIONAL, at 8 wait states: open VIDEOD.O88
   and play GasGas24.V88 (the 24 fps Gas Gas Gas)
   and the Bad Carrot encode to the end, each by
   its Open button - a double-click on a .V88
   opens the ordinary player instead. The info
   card (I) then has four extra lines: Heap, Ring,
   Lead and Dry. Photograph them for each file,
   with whether it played smoothly. That is how
   much room the real playback had, which the
   model is checked against.

6. OPTIONAL: VIDDISK L again WHILE A MODULE PLAYS
   (Tracker or MIDIRack through the Sound Blaster).
   Listen for clicks. -> the next VDLALL number.

7. THE PLAY'S PROFILE (the 286) - THIS ROUND'S
   ASK. VIDEOD.O88 now times every frame it
   draws and, when the play stops, saves the
   times as VPPROF1.DAT (then 2, 3 ... 9) - in
   the folder it was playing from, so look
   beside the player AND beside the videos.
   What they are for: fitting the encoder's
   model to what this machine really does, so a
   profile's defaults make what you now get by
   raising --peak.
   Bus at 8 wait states. Copy VIDEOD.O88 next
   to the videos, open it, and play each video
   to the END by its Open button, one after the
   other, closing nothing in between - a
   double-click on a .V88 opens the ordinary
   player, which saves nothing. Note which
   VPPROFn.DAT is which video (they number in
   the order played) and whether each played
   smoothly. Bring back every VPPROFn.DAT.
   A speaker play saves nothing: use the card.

8. A PLAY THAT BREAKS - THIS ROUND'S OTHER ASK.
   VIDEOR.O88 is VIDEOD that also writes down
   every chunk it reads off the disk - where in
   the file, how many bytes, and a checksum of
   them as they arrived - and, if the play stops
   on "a damaged super-packet", where the player
   was. Copy it next to the video that breaks,
   open it, play that video MUTED until it
   breaks, press Esc, and bring back the
   VPPROFn.DAT it saves. It says whether the
   disk handed over the wrong bytes, and which.
   Same wait states as the play that broke.

IF THE MACHINE FREEZES at "probing LOADALL": reset,
boot again and press M instead of L.

Bring the .TXT files back. FTP them off, or
photograph every page: Space / PgDn / PgUp page.

WHAT EACH ONE SETS
------------------
  VIDBENCH   the decode cost and the latch copy
  VIDDISK R  the disk rate and its curve as the
             decode takes 0/25/50/75% of a frame
  VIDDISK X  what banking video in XMS costs
  VIDDISK L  the same copies by LOADALL, and the
             in-place read the bank would use
  VIDDISK E  the same for the PicoMEM's EMS
  VIDSND     the sound the profile may ask for
             (PCM 22 kHz, 8 kHz, 4-bit ADPCM)
  VIDEOD     the headroom a real file had
  VIDEOR     where a play that breaks went wrong

On the 286, VIDSND's 25/50/75% rows are timed for an
8088 and mean less there; its interrupt counts and
the ADPCM row are what matter. Its row with
interrupts on is not run on a PicoMEM, whose
interrupt may not be re-entered.
