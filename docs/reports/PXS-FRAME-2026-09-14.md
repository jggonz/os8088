## 1. What a wave-1 frame is, and the two frames measured

The Flat and Wire rungs on the static ladders (SPEC.md 97.3), the cast of
97.2 with the four bodies patched a column, the Δ-fill of 97.5 and each
backend's present — and NOTHING of what the table also charges: no sprite
candidates or posts (3 sprites, 45–75 posts D), no weapon, no HUD, no
simulation beyond the player's own step (the 4.60 ms tick is wave 3's).

**Two frames a row, because one number is not a frame.** The **FULL
REPAINT** pokes `px_force_all`'s memory at every stop (`pxslib.force_all_poke`:
the seven history arrays and the byte), so every column writes ceiling, wall
and floor and the present sends all 80 rows — the dearest frame there is,
and **the one the promise is asserted on**. The **TURN** frame steps the
heading by `PX_TURN` at every stop with `px_dirty` set and nothing forced,
so the Δ-fill writes what a turn changes and the present sends the rows it
touched — the frame a player sees. The first take of this file poked
`px_force` alone and called it a full repaint; that byte only bypasses the
idle predicate, and what it composed was every WALL and no ceiling or floor
(the Flat column then wrote its wall unconditionally, which 97.5's
unchanged-column skip has since removed — today that frame composes nothing
on a still eye). What it measured was, to 0.4 ms, the TURN frame below,
which is why the promise's margin shrank in this file and not the machine.

On the two pinned scenes the row range of both frames is 80 rows: scene A
has the corridor's side wall at the view's edge and scene B the door's
jamb, each taller than the view, so `min(top) = 0` and `max(bot) = 79`
whatever moved. It is not a property of the scenes: the range is ONE
min/max over all columns, any column nearer than 2.5 tiles spans the band,
and every scene in E1M1 has one that a turn moves — `pxslib.present_rows`,
the host's model of `pxcomp.inc`'s rule, reads 74 61 63 63 64 80 80 80 80
rows over nine successive turns from A and 73 74 77 77 80 80 80 80 80 from
B (best case 61, median 80), so the present is a constant 21.2 ms on every
frame a player sees and the `rows presented` column is ASSERTED against
that model rather than noted (SPEC.md 97.5, 97.10).

Against the units of `docs/reports/PXS-FRAME-2026-09-13.md`:

- **The cast is 4,830 clk a ray** (the median of the four) against the
  units' 1,094 + 9.3 × 128.2 + 1,294.5 = **3,581**: +1,250 a ray, and it
  does not depend on the resolution or on what the compose does. The far
  call and its `retf` are ~90 of it; the rest is the package's column setup
  and hit being longer than the bench's — `px_col` and `px_qcur` through
  memory, the per-quadrant tables indexed by `SI`, the pushes around the
  patches, the jamb test and the mirror in the hit. **Textured inherits all
  of it**: the cast is the same code. `CAST_RESID` = 1,250 in
  `tools/pxsframe.py`.
- **The Flat compose is ~1,400 a column over its stores and ladder

  driver of wave 2 replaces the shape, so it is the Flat rung's and not the
  design's. `FLAT_RESID` = 1,400. The turn frame's compose (2,798 / 2,495 a
  column) is what a turn's Δ-fill actually writes on scene A: roughly the
  wall and one ground run a column.
- **The present is the units' figure**: 101,200 against 5,120 × 19.4 =
  99,300 plus the row loop.
- **The loop is 7,900**: ten `OSAPI_KEY_DOWN`s (not eight — with nothing
  held every `jc` falls through to the second reader) = 5,410, plus `int

  `pit_now` and the selector.
