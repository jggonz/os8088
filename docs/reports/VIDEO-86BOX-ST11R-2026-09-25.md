# Video Player benches on 86Box: an ST11R and a DMA disk

**A measurement, not a description.** Taken 2026-09-25 by the owner, in 86Box
on a Ryzen 7 9800X3D host, off the field hard disks `VIDCGA-ST11R.VHD` and
`VIDHERC-ST11R.VHD` cut at `c5d68c93` (`make vidfieldhd`). It is true of that
tree and that emulator and is not maintained against later ones. The
MartyPC figures beside it are this session's, off the same benches.

**It is 86Box, not iron.** Two of its findings are about the EMULATOR, and
they are marked. The disk is 86Box's model of an ST-238R on a Seagate ST11R;
the owner reports 86Box has been close on disk speed before, and every disk
figure here wants the ST-225 on the 5150 to confirm it.

The machines (`86box.cfg`, both): `ibmpc82`, 8088 at 4.77 MHz, 256 KB plus a
SixPakPlus's 384, `st506_xt_st11_r` with a 615/4/26 drive, a Sound Blaster
2.0, and a CGA on one and a Hercules on the other.

## What they found

1. **A DMA disk barely notices the decoder.** The silent player's ceiling -
   `READ_SEQ` streaming 32 KB calls for 5 s inside an `FSXF_RATE` bracket
   whose 30 Hz hook holds n% of every period, interrupts on:

   | hook holds | 86Box ST11R (CGA / Herc) | MartyPC XT-IDE |
   |---|---|---|
   | 0% | 300.7 / 329.1 KB/s | 198.3 |
   | 25% | 284.8 / 319.9 | 150.2 |
   | 50% | 253.2 / 275.1 | 99.1 |
   | 75% | 171.6 / 217.5 | 49.0 |
   | 50%, interrupts off | 253.2 / 278.5 | 98.0 |

   VIDSND's whole-track reads say the same with the sound interrupt burning
   instead: **148, 147, 145, 142** tracks in 5 s at 0/25/50/75%, where the
   XT-IDE reads 90, 65, 41, 17. The CPU-copied controller gives the disk
   exactly the time the decode leaves it; the DMA one keeps going through
   it. BADAPPLE needs ~61 KB/s. So on an ST11-class disk streaming is not the
   constraint at any decode share, and on a CPU-copied disk (MartyPC's
   XT-IDE, and whatever the PicoMEM does) a 75% decode leaves less than the
   stream needs - which the player survives only because the decode
   AVERAGES far below its worst frame.

   Interrupts on or off in the hook made no difference on either. On a
   DMA controller the completion interrupt waiting out a burn costs a
   fraction of a revolution per transfer, and that is below this bench's
   resolution.

2. **ADPCM4 plays on 86Box's Sound Blaster 2.0**: 150 interrupts in 5 s at
   22,050 Hz / 368 bytes, the same count as PCM8. MartyPC's card reads 0.
   So wave 4's 4-bit path can be checked on 86Box where MartyPC cannot check
   it, and the 5150 with the ST-225 has no card at all.

3. **86Box times the CGA and the Hercules identically** *(the emulator)*.
   Every VIDBENCH row agrees across the two machines to 0.5%. That includes
   the raw stores (`rep movsw` of 8000 bytes to the screen: 33.81 ms on both
   against 21.6 to RAM), where MartyPC gives the Hercules RAM speed and the
   CGA 30.2. 86Box applies one wait-state model to both cards, so it cannot
   answer the question VIDBENCH exists for; the 5150 can.

4. **The worst frame fits on both**, in thousandths of a 30 fps period:

   | | XDC's code | ours |
   |---|---|---|
   | 86Box, either card | 751 | 877 / 874 |
   | MartyPC CGA | 704 | 778 |
   | MartyPC Herc | 801 | 764 |

   86Box's heavier screen writes fall harder on our decoder than on XDC's
   (TRONDISC 396: 29.2 ms against 22.8), which is wave 0's finding - the
   worst frames cost up to 1.28x XDC's own code - read on a slower screen.
   It is under 1000 everywhere.

5. **The rate clock is exact**: 150 periods in 91 ticks, the BIOS count
   moving with `[ticks]`, 16,483 periods per 10,000 ticks against 16,478 -
   the same as MartyPC to the unit. 142 hook calls against MartyPC's 134:
   86Box ran the long hooks shorter, so fewer periods fell inside one.

6. **The disk:** a whole 26-sector track in 34.3 ms (379 KB/s, two
   revolutions at 3,600 rpm) and one sector in 2.7 ms. `READ_SEQ` inside the
   bracket gets 80-87% of that. `READ_AT` grows 133 ms per MB of offset
   (MartyPC: 142 - it is the CPU walking the chain either way), and
   `READ_SEQ` is flat from 0 to 12 MB with no call under cylinder 16. A
   32 KB `READ_SEQ` at the desktop reads 164.8 ms on the CGA machine and
   96.1 ms on the Hercules one, against ~100 ms inside the bracket on both.
   That gap is desktop-side, and SPEC.md 12.8.5.2's progress box is the
   suspect - the player never pays it, being in a bracket - but it is
   unexplained here.

7. **The Sound Blaster answered on IRQ 5.** The same `86box.cfg` carries an
   `[ST-11M (MFM) #1]` section at IRQ 5 and the ST11R's own line is not in
   it. If the hard disk card and the sound card share IRQ 5 on a real
   machine, wave 4's frame interrupt and the disk will collide. It did not
   bite here: VIDSND's ceiling rows read the disk while the card
   interrupted.
