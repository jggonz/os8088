# Video Player benches on an 86Box 486DX2/66, and the 286 again

**A measurement, not a description.** Taken by the owner in 86Box and
handed over on 2026-10-07, off the benches `make vid486` builds (SPEC.md
98.2.3.1) at `6bdc1b1`. It is true of those emulated machines and
is not maintained against later trees.

- **486**: the owner's own 86Box 486, a DX2/66 with an ISA SVGA, IDE (63
  sectors, 16 heads) and a Sound Blaster on 220h / IRQ 5 (DSP 4.05).
  NOT `vm/486-video`: that profile's `VID486.VHD` answered `Disk error`
  from its boot sector with A: set to a 1.44 MB and to a 320 KB drive, so
  the benches went onto the owner's own disk. That failure is open.
- **286**: the owner's 286 (IDE at 63 sectors, 32 heads), the same card's
  bus as `docs/reports/VIDEO-86BOX-286-2026-09-26.md` - its raw stores
  agree with that run to the microsecond.

## VIDBENCH: the card

8,000 bytes a row, the mean of 8:

| row | 286 | 486 | MartyPC's XT VGA (no wait states) |
|---|---|---|---|
| `rep movsb` to the screen | 8.36 ms | 3.30 ms | 30.2 ms |
| `rep movsw` to the screen | 7.32 ms | 3.12 ms | 22.0 ms |
| `rep movsb` to RAM | 2.10 ms | 0.37 ms | 30.2 ms |
| `rep movsw` to RAM | 1.05 ms | 0.18 ms | 22.0 ms |
| `rep movsb` screen to RAM (a READ) | 8.36 ms | **8.16 ms** | 30.2 ms |
| `rep movsb` in mode 12h | 8.36 ms | 3.30 ms | 30.2 ms |
| latch copy, screen to screen | 14.63 ms | **11.04 ms** | 30.2 ms |

The 486's CPU is 5.7x the 286's on RAM and its card 2.3x on stores, and a
READ from the card is the same ~1 us a byte on both. So the flipped
player's latch copy (a read and a write a byte) is 3.5x a store on the 486
and 2x on the 286.

Frames, VIDBENCH's synthetic set (`os88vid.py synthxdv`) decoded by the
player's own decoder to the screen, against the encoder's model for them
(MartyPC's CGA 5150, which that model fits to 0.4%):

| frame | bytes | model, cycles | 286 | x model | 486 | x model |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| SYNTH 7 (slices of 16) | 2,400 | 56,619 | 2,607 us | 4.55 | 1,055 us | 11.24 |
| SL40 x60 | 2,400 | 49,137 | 2,377 | 4.33 | 1,010 | 10.19 |
| RUN16 x150 | 2,400 | 47,197 | 2,663 | 3.71 | 1,035 | 9.55 |
| RUN40 x60 | 2,400 | 38,188 | 2,380 | 3.36 | 977 | 8.19 |
| P4 x300 | 1,200 | 32,292 | 1,516 | 4.46 | 558 | 12.13 |
| P6 x200 | 1,200 | 30,111 | 1,427 | 4.42 | 546 | 11.56 |
| P2 x400 | 800 | 26,941 | 1,200 | 4.70 | 395 | 14.30 |
| P1 x400 | 400 | 20,678 | 900 | 4.81 | 233 | 18.61 |

The small frames run further ahead of the model on the 486 because their
cost is instructions, which the 486 does at 66 MHz, and the large ones are
bounded by the card. The worst frame was 79 per mille of a 30 fps period
on the 286 and 31 on the 486.

## VIDDISK: the disk

The silent player's ceiling, 32 KB `READ_SEQ` with a 30 Hz hook holding n%
of every period, KB/s:

| hook holds | 286 | 486 |
|---|---|---|
| 0% | 1,318.3 | 4,151.1 |
| 25% | 1,023.9 | 3,156.5 |
| 50% | 684.7 | 2,191.9 |
| 75% | 348.1 | 1,095.8 |
| 50%, interrupts off | 686.6 | 2,190.9 |

Both are disks the CPU copies: the stream gets what the decode leaves. A
32 KB `READ_SEQ` is 27 ms on the 286 and under one 55 ms tick (the
bench's clock) on the 486; an `int 13h` track is 23 ms and 8 ms.

## VIDSND: the card

Both: DSP 4.05 at 220h, IRQ 5; 150 / 300 / 150 interrupts in 5 s at
22 kHz/735, 8 kHz/134 and ADPCM4 22 kHz/368 (the 286 151 at the first).
Tracks read in 5 s with the sound interrupt burning 0 / 25 / 50 / 75%:
286 220 / 207 / 198 / 184, 486 659 / 653 / 648 / 644.

## A play

LX48F (Last Exile 0:48-0:58, lossless Mode X at 24 fps, PCM8 22 kHz
ahead, flipped, 48 KB frames, 357 KB/s) on the 486 under `VIDEOD.O88`:
**drew 239 of 239, 0 stalls, 0 pauses, 0 late**, 180 ticks of 181; heap
473K, ring 8 slots of 350K, lead 6 at frame 11, hook gap 2, dry 0.

## What it decided

SPEC.md 98.2.3.2: a `486` profile at speed 10 (1,950,000 B/s, 500% / 850%,
`disk_at` off the table above), and `286-vga` re-read at speed 4.5 (225% /
375%) where it had been 6. SPEC.md 98.2.3.3, the same day: the frame
table above is FITTED per machine (`cyc_us`, every row back to 0.4%), so
runs and pixels are each priced at what the machine took - one `speed`
put a frame of runs that the encoder called 83% of a period at ~111% on
the 286 - and both profiles carry their latch row (`lcopy_us`) and the
286 its disk rows (`disk_at`).
