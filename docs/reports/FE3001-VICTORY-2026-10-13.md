# The Packard Bell Victory 286's 8-bit wait states (Western Digital FE3001)

**A measurement, not a description.** Taken 2026-10-12/13 by the owner on the
**Packard Bell Victory 286** (docs/FIELD-MACHINES.md): a 16 MHz AMD 286, the
onboard Paradise PVGA1A, a PicoMEM 1.x serving C: on the PicoMEM's
multi-sector firmware, and Western Digital's **FE3600** chipset (FE3001,
FE3010, FE3021 and FE3031; FE3010B, a 32 MHz oscillator, and Packard Bell's
own PB82C01 seen on the board). The instrument is `PMDMAFE.O88`, PMDMA with a
W page built from the `picomem-fe3001` branch (85512291), copied onto an
install of `picomem` at 0e16450c. True of that machine and that tree; not
maintained.

## What the FE3001 data sheet says

From Western Digital's FE3001 and FE3001A data sheets (the 1991 System Logic
data book, bitsavers `components/westernDigital/`):
- **The bus clock is 8 MHz.** With the high-speed clock selected SYSCLK is
  PROCLK / 4, and PROCLK is the 32 MHz oscillator.
- **R4 sets the 8-bit wait states.** An 8-bit memory or I/O cycle is Ts + Tc
  plus R4 wait states of two PROCLKs each (62.5 ns). Appendix A recommends
  **R4 = 10** at 16 MHz, which is an 8 MHz AT's cycle.
- **The registers are write-only.**
  - 063h is the speed select: bit 2 the CPU clock, bit 3 the DMA speed, bit 6
    stops the clocks, bit 7 locks.
  - 072h is the pointer and 073h the value. The register number and the
    value both go in bits 7, 6, 3 and 2.
- **A cycle ends on a rising edge of SYSCLK**, and the chip adds a wait state
  where it must to do so.

## What the board did

Four sweeps (PMWAIT1-4) agree to within 0.3%. Card memory is the PicoMEM's
disk transfer buffer; ns a byte is net of the same access to RAM.

| R4 | card read, ns a byte | card write | screen read | card I/O over board I/O |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| as the BIOS set it | 915 | 856 | 924 | 0 |
| 10 (WD's recommendation), and 9 | 651 | 593 | 852 | 122 |
| 8, and 7 | 523 | 459 | 852 | 265 |
| 6 | refused: 5,108 wrong reads, the same in every sweep | | | |

- **The BIOS runs slower than WD's own recommendation.** It leaves 263 ns a
  byte more than R4 = 10, about four wait states. The registers cannot be read,
  so whether that is R4 = 14 or a longer command delay (R3) is not known.
- **An odd R4 is the next even one.** 7 measures as 8 and 9 measures as 10:
  two wait states are one SYSCLK, and the cycle ends on its edge. 8 is the
  floor, and 6 is a hard failure, not a marginal one.
- **The VGA and the card's ports stretch their own cycles.** A screen read
  stops improving at R4 = 10 (852 ns), and so does an I/O read of the card,
  while port 61h (the board's) goes on improving to R4 = 8.
- **Every device on the 8-bit bus ran at R4 = 7** in the owner's use: the
  PicoMEM's disk, the VGA, the keyboard controller, an NE2000 network
  transfer, the parallel port, a serial mouse and video playback. The clock
  chip (DS1287) took a time set at R4 = 7, read back correctly after a reboot
  and a power-off, and there was no CMOS checksum error.

## The disk at R4 = 7

VIDDISK's silent player, 32 KB `READ_SEQ`, KB/s:

| hook | stock firmware, BIOS bus | multi-sector firmware, BIOS bus | multi-sector, R4 = 7 | 286-fast's IDE, BIOS bus |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0% | 684.7 | 755.1 | **1,113.5** | 729.5 |
| 25% | 518.3 | 575.9 | **831.9** | 684.7 |
| 50% | 345.5 | 379.8 | **563.1** | 486.3 |
| 75% | 172.7 | 189.9 | **278.5** | 294.3 |

With interrupts off it is 561.0 at 50%. One sector through int 13h takes 915
us, down from 1,831. By the encoder's rule (0.9 x the 50% row) this machine is
~519,000 B/s. Every encode made for `286-fast` (448,000) played clean,
including one pushed to 460 KB/s.
