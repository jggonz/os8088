# VIDDISK X and R on the owner's real 286: what an XMS copy costs

**A measurement, not a description.** Taken 2026-10-08 by the owner on a real
286 (not 86Box) with ~3.3 MB of extended memory and a ~100 MB 2.5-inch laptop
IDE drive that was nearly full and FRAGMENTED when STREAM.DAT was written -
the owner's own words: "likely a worst case". The VIDDISK floppy was the one
built on branch `video-xms` (VIDDISK's `X` arrived at `0ddbfc9`). It is
true of that machine and is not maintained against later trees. The three
reports are reproduced whole at the end.

## What it settles

**The 286's `int 15h AH=87h` copy: 0.345 ms a KB, ~2.9 MB/s - docs/plans/
VIDEO-XMS-PLAN.md 3.2's middle column, and NOT its 1 ms/KB one.** Up and down
cost the same, and the cost is a straight line in the size:

| bytes a call | up | down | |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4 KB | 1,804 us | 1,805 us | |
| 8 KB | 3,154 | 3,168 | |
| 16 KB | 5,868 | 5,870 | |
| 32 KB | 11,301 | 11,282 | **0.345 ms/KB** |

Fitted: **~0.45 ms fixed a call** (the protected-mode entry and the CPU reset
back) **+ 0.339 ms a KB**. On a 286 the whole call is interrupts-off, so a
32 KB piece holds IRQ0 and the card for **11.3 ms** - under half of a
512-byte block at 22 kHz (23 ms), VIDEO-XMS-PLAN 4.5's line; a 16 KB piece
is 5.9 ms for ~4% more cost.

**The disk is not slow: 736 KB/s idle**, and `READ_SEQ` at 0 MB and at
12 MB alike (48.06 ms a 32 KB call), so the fragmentation did not show at
this granularity. Under VIDDISK's 30 Hz hook (interrupts ON) it streamed
**730 / 685 / 486 / 294 KB/s at 0 / 25 / 50 / 75%**; 50% with interrupts
off, 442. That is not a CPU-bound curve (which would fall as 75/50/25% of
idle): the drive reads ahead while the CPU is busy, so up to ~25% load the
media binds and past it the CPU's ~0.85-1.0 ms a KB does.

**Banking a byte costs the fill 14%, not the copy's whole price**:
`READ_SEQ` alone 735.9 KB/s, `READ_SEQ` + a 32 KB copy up behind each
call 633.5 KB/s - an extra 0.22 ms a KB against the copy's 0.345, the
difference being the drive reading on while the CPU copies.

## What it means for the bank

`tools/os88vidbuf.py` re-run on the owner's lossless Last Exile
(`05-320FS.V88`, 363 KB/s mean) with THIS machine's copy (0.345) and disk
CPU (0.85 and 1.0 ms/KB bracket the curve above), a 64 KB drive cache - and
86Box's mr286 decode (`286-vga`), because no VIDBENCH has been run on this
machine and its decode speed is the one thing not measured:

| disk CPU | ring alone (today's player) | 1 MB bank | 2 MB | 3 MB (what it has free) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.85 ms/KB | stalls 0.1 s at ANY disk rate | 431 KB/s, 3.1 s prefill | 431, 5.5 s | 418, 8.3 s |
| 1.0 ms/KB | stalls 0.5 s at ANY disk rate | stalls 0.1 s | 485, 4.9 s | 470, 7.4 s |

On this machine the ring alone is CPU-bound in the bursts - the disk takes
~0.9 ms of CPU a KB exactly when the decode wants it - and **a bank of 2-3 MB
turns that into a clean play off a disk needing 420-485 KB/s, which this one
streams at 736.** A chunk drained from XMS costs 0.345 ms a KB where the disk
costs ~0.9: the bank gives the bursts their CPU back.

## Not settled here

- **This 286's decode speed.** The table prices frames at 86Box's mr286; a
  slower CPU moves every cell. VIDBENCH on this machine, or playing
  `05-320FS.V88` on it with today's player and reading the card's stalls,
  checks the "ring alone stalls" column directly.
- **EMS on a 286** (an 8-bit card's copy, VIDEO-XMS-PLAN 10.3): this machine
  ran X and R, not E.
