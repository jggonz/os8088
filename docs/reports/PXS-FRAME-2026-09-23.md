# PIXELSTEIN 3D — wave 4's frame: the status bar, the states and the timedemo, measured

**A measurement, not a description.** Taken 2026-09-23 on branch
`game/pixelstein` (wave 4 and its first review round on top of wave 3), on
MartyPC's cycle-exact 5150 machines through `tests/pixelstein.py`,
`tests/pxsstate.py`, `tests/pxshud.py` and one breakpoint script — the same
method as the four reports before it (`PXS-FRAME-2026-09-13.md`, `-14.md`,
`-21.md`, `-22.md`): a breakpoint on `px_frame_begin`, the median of twelve
consecutive cycle deltas, a FULL REPAINT frame with `px_force_all` poked at
every stop, a TURN frame with the heading stepped by `PX_TURN`, and the
FINISHED frame — the full repaint with the world's simulation running, the
player invulnerable, the pose put back at every stop. SPEC.md 97.13 is what
these numbers are quoted in; 97.1 carries the fork's re-measured line.

## 1. What a wave-4 frame adds

Wave 3's frame plus: the status bar's bookkeeping (`px_hud_poll` once a
simulation step, `px_hud_draw` once a frame — a ten-byte snapshot of the
bar's inputs compared with the one this page was drawn from, and the changed
fields alone written when it differs), `px_timers` (the grin, the message,
the card hold) and the state dispatch. A frame whose bar did not change
writes no bar byte. The review round (r1) changed what the bookkeeping costs,
not what it draws: the poll returns at once when a frame is already owed, the
second page's compare and the page-1 copies are gone off Mode X, and each
rewritten field presents its own rectangle.

## 3. Why r1 moved these rows by ≤ 0.1 ms, and what it moved in play

The unit prices, by breakpoint (entry to exit, guest cycles, the bracket at
the default, eight samples each, every sample within 3 cycles):

| path | cycles |
|---|---:|
| `px_hud_poll`, a step that owes nothing (snapshot + one ten-byte compare) | **1,049** |
| `px_hud_poll`, a step that already owes a frame (review r1's early exit) | **139** |
| `px_hud_draw` on an unchanged snapshot, to `px_present`'s entry | **975** (~915 of it the draw) |

`tests/pixelstein.py` pokes its force and its heading at `px_frame_begin`'s
entry — AFTER the frame's steps — so every poll in its rows takes the
1,049-cycle path, and what r1 took out of its frames is the draw's page-1
copies (~500 cycles, ~0.1 ms): A's full repaint 111.2 → 111.1. A player's
step is different: `px_step_move` owes the view before `px_hud_poll` runs,
so a walking or turning frame's two or three polls are 139 cycles each where
the first cut's were ~1,150, and the frame's bar bookkeeping is ~1.2k cycles
(~0.25 ms) where it was ~3.5–3.8k.

## 5. The timedemo

**Superseded by §8**: the timedemo now draws the weapon and reads 10.1.
`tests/pxsstate.py` leg i on `os8088_5150_cga_gla`, two runs in review r1:
**81 frames in 136 ticks = 10.8 fps**, pinned to Textured Low res Size 64
with Auto and Size 48 poked in force beforehand (the card's rung line reads
"Textured  Low res 64"). Wave 4's first cut read 135–136 ticks unpinned at
the default. ~92 ms a frame against the turn row's ~101 — and this section's
first reason for that (no world tick, no bar poll) was wrong: the turn row
freezes the world too. §8 has the measured reason. On QEMU (a
386, `wave4r1-qemu-modex-timedemo.png`) the same card reads 81 frames in 3
ticks — a count, not a timing.

So the first cut's 10.8 had three causes, none of them the one §5 gave:
**the weapon** (~5 ms of compose: `px_weapon_check` drew it in PLAY alone —
fixed, the timedemo draws it now), **the walk segment** (16 of the 80

played frame reads the keys once — `px_keys_read` 5,816 clk, 1.22 ms, eleven

long arcs do the rest: a turn frame moves with the heading, turn A's from
91 to 107 ms. The About card now quotes 8.7 (the finished frame, scene A,
the world running — what the promise and the fork are written on) and 10.1
(the timedemo, a walk and turn of a frozen world), and says which is which.

The frame is **bimodal**: a chaser step is ~10.3 ms in both builds, and a
~135 ms frame carries two steps or three by where the tick edges fall, so
the frames sit near 132 and near 143 and a median of eight picks a mode.
Wave 3's "133.2 (+19.9)" and wave 4's "143.3 (+29.0)" are the two modes, not
a 9 ms regression. What wave 4 does add is ~0.3 ms a step (`px_timers` and
`px_hud_poll`), ~0.9 ms on the chasers' ~2.4 steps a frame, and the mean
moved 2.7 ms, part of it the extra step a frame that crossing a tick edge
more often costs. `tests/pxsact.py` prints the eight frames, their mean and
their range beside the median since review r2.
