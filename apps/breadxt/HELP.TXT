BREADBOARD XT 1.0 - FIRST STEPS

Breadboard XT calculates educational DC, four-state digital logic and bounded
RC transients, including a pin-level NE555 timer. It is not SPICE. It does not
model transistor internals, heat, damage, LED avalanche, power consumption or
realistic output-drive limits. Calculation may run slower than real time.

THE BOARD
Columns are 1 through 30. In each column, rows a-e form a five-hole strip and
rows f-j form another. The center trench separates them. Adjacent columns
are separate. Every power rail breaks between columns 15 and 16. A break is
real until a wire bridges it; FLASHER explicitly bridges the bottom negative
rail. Crossings are not junctions: wires connect only through endpoint holes
and their internal strips. Solid marks show the selected physical strip;
dotted marks show other strips joined to its electrical net.

One inserted lead fits in each hole, including wire ends. Put a wire into a
neighboring hole on the same strip rather than on top of a part lead. Probes
consume no hole. Moving/deleting a part leaves wires in their physical holes;
that complete part edit is undoable. Stable IDs let a pin probe follow a
moved part. Deleting its target unbinds the probe; Undo restores it.

BUILD A CIRCUIT
Use Parts or F2 to choose a part. Click its destination holes and Enter to
commit the preview; Esc cancels without changing the document. DIP packages
span e/f. The notch and pin-1 mark show orientation; R rotates 180 degrees.
W chooses Wire: click explicit endpoint holes, optionally use B for up to
two orthogonal bends, then Enter. Bends change the picture, not connectivity.
Use V to select, M to move, E to edit value, T to edit parameter and Del to
remove. Edit also provides wire rerouting and recoloring.

RUN AND MEASURE
Space runs/pauses; period advances one numerical step. Simulate also provides
Run/Pause/Step/Reset. A step defaults to 1 ms of SIMULATED time. Time has
100 us resolution; the displayed clock belongs to the circuit, not the host.
An XT may calculate slowly. Editing pauses and marks readings stale; Run/Step
recompiles the changed circuit. Pause retains inspectable state. Reset restores
initial capacitor, latch and clock state, and clears scope capture.

P chooses Probe; Tab changes channel A/B. A hole gives voltage relative to
actual ground plus logic 0/1/X/Z. A part-pin binding follows stable identity.
A supported branch gives current from pin 1 to pin 2 in microamps. A source
with positive pin 1 therefore reads NEGATIVE while delivering energy and
positive while absorbing capacitor backdrive. Current is not node voltage.
Pots and ICs have no single two-terminal branch current and show N/A. Floating,
unknown, stale, failed and paused readings are labeled, never invented 0 V.
Click an active error in the status line to locate its part/net when available.

F4 opens Scope, a separate view on short displays. Its two channels contain
calculated voltages and simulation timestamps. +/- changes time scale; G
cycles free/rising/falling trigger on A. Monochrome traces have different
patterns. Pause holds capture; Reset clears it. Frequency/duty come from edge
timestamps when enough cycles exist. Insufficient cycles and undersampling
are labeled. The first 555 startup pulse differs from settled timing.

VALUES AND MODELS
R/Pot accepts decimal ohms: 330, 330ohm, 10k, 68k, 655.35k. Resistance is
100..655350 ohms in exact 10-ohm increments; 333ohm is rejected, not rounded.
Cap accepts nF/uF: 10nF, 10uF, 0.1uF; range 1..60000 nF. Supply accepts
4500..6000 mV or 4.5..6 V; use 5V. Exactly ONE ground-referenced supply is
supported. A second supply receives a diagnostic instead of an invented
current-sharing result. Clock accepts us/ms/s or raw 100 us units; its period
is 2..60000 units. Fractions have at most three decimal digits. No signs,
exponents, arbitrary SPICE suffixes, unsupported precision or silent overflow.

T edits parameters: pot wiper 0..1000 permille; capacitor initial voltage
0..6000 mV; input 0/1/2/3 means 0/1/X/Z; clock duty 1..999 permille. A pot's
zero-length endpoint segment is an ideal connection, not division by zero.
LED pin 1 is anode, pin 2 cathode. Its forward model is 2 V plus 10 ohms;
reverse blocks ideally. 5 V through 330 ohms gives about 8.824 mA, compared
with 9.091 mA for an ideal 2 V drop alone. There is NO hidden series limiter.
Direct all-LED paths between ideal rails are diagnosed even when current is
under 30 mA; a resistor in parallel does not provide series limiting. All
branch currents, including total source current, must stay within +/-30 mA.

An open or unpowered LED stub can have zero current and a FLOAT/Z voltage.
Real resistor/capacitor references and stored capacitor state still matter.
Near-zero-current cycles made only of LEDs can maintain ambiguous clamps;
this bounded model conservatively diagnoses FLOAT instead of choosing an
arbitrary voltage. That does not claim the physical circuit is insoluble.

HC00/04/08/32/86 use verified 14-pin PDIP maps, GND 7 and VCC 14. Always wire
power. LOW is at/below 0.3 VCC, HIGH at/above 0.7 VCC; intermediate voltage
is X. Undriven Z is consumed as unknown, never silently zero. Conflicting
outputs, unsupported analog/combinational feedback and event exhaustion
diagnose. These are educational ideal logic outputs, not real drive limits.
IC outputs supply their loads as ideal rail drivers. Their consumption is
omitted from power-pin current; the DC source reading covers the external
network connected to that source node, rather than a complete IC power budget.

NE555 pins: 1 GND, 2 TRIG, 3 OUT, 4 RESET, 5 CONT, 6 THRES, 7 DISCH, 8 VCC.
RESET below 0.7 V dominates, including with unwired TRIG/THRES. Otherwise
TRIG below VCC/3 sets; then THRES above 2 VCC/3 resets; otherwise hold. OUT
is an ideal rail driver; DISCH changes the actual connected RC network. CONT
supports normal open/default or a capacitor to ground. External control drive
is not modeled and receives a diagnostic. No stored waveform runs the timer.

EXAMPLES TO TRY
LEDTEST: 5 V, 330 ohms, red LED. Expect about 8.824 mA; reverse to block.
DIVIDER: two 10k resistors. Midpoint 2.500 V, series current 250 uA.
POT: 10k pot at 500 permille. Wiper 2.500 V; T moves toward either end.
SWITCH: closed switch lights the limited LED; Space toggles the selection.
LOGIC: powered HC00, two HIGH inputs -> LOW first NAND output. A LOW input
makes it HIGH. Other unused inputs remain Z/X.
BROKEN: LED directly across 5 V. Expect a series-resistor diagnostic. Rewire
with a real 330-ohm series resistor, then Run again.
RC: 10k and 10uF. Time constant 0.1 s. At 0.1 s expect about 3.15 V at a
1 ms step (continuous ideal 3.161 V); a smaller step converges toward ideal.
FLASHER: 5 V, RA 10k, RB 68k, timing C 10uF, CONT bypass 10nF and 330-ohm
LED load. Channels show OUT and timing capacitor. Settled ideal estimates
are 0.988 Hz and 53.4% HIGH duty. Change RB, break its timing wire, ground
RESET or remove power: the calculated operation must change.
All examples are ordinary editable BBX documents, not canned demonstrations.

FILES, RECOVERY AND KEYS
Ctrl-N/O/S = New/Open/Save. File also has Save As and examples. Unsaved work
uses native Save/Discard/Cancel. A failed/canceled load leaves the old source
intact; failed saves keep it dirty. Native replacement writes a new FAT chain
before committing its directory entry, then frees the old chain. No delete-
before-save operation exists. A power loss during final cleanup can leave
an old chain for file-system recovery; keep another copy of valuable work.

Ctrl-Z/Y = Undo/Redo, retaining 16 complete object edits. New edits after
Undo clear redo. Arrows pan; PageUp/Down pan four rows. F1 Help, F2 palette,
F3 inspector, F4 scope. Esc returns/cancels; Enter advances Help pages. CGA
uses switchable readable views rather than shrinking VGA text. State,
polarity, selection and traces remain understandable without color.

LIMITS AND SOURCES
One 30-column board; 64 parts; 128 wires; two bends per wire; 64 active nets;
24 unknown analog nodes; 16 undo commands; two 256-sample scope channels.
Default capture cadence 20 ms spans 5.12 s. Node domain 0..6 V; branch current
-30..30 mA. Bounded pivots, LED passes and events diagnose difficult circuits.
An unfinished circuit may still be saved if its source is structurally valid.

Calculation is much slower than real time on a 4.77 MHz XT. The measured
reference flasher advances about 2.5 simulated milliseconds per real second
at a 4 ms timestep. Larger timesteps give quicker previews with less timing
accuracy. A 256 KB machine can run the full package alone, but the measured
session left only 3 KB free; close other programs before opening it there.

Breadboard XT 1.0 is an original os8088 application. David Shah's GPL
Breadboard Simulator inspired the workflow; none of its code, models or art
is incorporated. Pin maps/behavior follow TI manufacturer datasheets. See
repository docs/breadxt/SOURCES.md and FORMAT.md for provenance and format.
