# Charter

Charter is a sturdy serif typeface drawn for os8088 by hand. Its capitals
are short and its lowercase letters large, the proportions of the early
Macintosh's system face, so a line of mostly small letters reads clearly
even on a low-resolution screen. It is a good face for reading a whole
document, and it holds up well on a CGA, where thin strokes fade.

![Font Viewer showing Charter with a sample typed in.](shot:charter-f88)

## Where it comes from

Charter was not traced from any existing font. It was drawn pixel by pixel
for this system, as one of its own faces, and it is covered by the same MIT
licence as the rest of os8088.

Every stem is two pixels wide. Two shapes differ from a classic serif face on
purpose: the capital `I` keeps its serifs, so it cannot be mistaken for a
lowercase `l` in a file name, and every figure is the same width, so numbers
line up in a column.

## What is in it

One style of 95 characters: the capital and small letters, the figures and
the punctuation of plain ASCII. There are no accented letters. Capitals are
8 pixels tall and the lowercase 6, in a 12-pixel line with 2 pixels between
lines. Letters are 4, 6 or 8 pixels wide.

## Using it

The face lives in `SYSTEM/FONTS` on the system disk. Double-click
**CHARTER.F88** to look at it in Font Viewer.

Microsoft Word, the C edition of Word and Scribe list it in the **Font** box
on their ribbon as **Charter**. Pick it and the whole document is shown in
Charter.
