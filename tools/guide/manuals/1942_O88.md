# 1942

1942 is a vertical shooter after the 1985 arcade and home game. You fly a
fighter up a scrolling ocean of islands and reefs, shooting down waves of
enemy planes and dodging their fire. Thirty-two stages make up the mission;
each one starts with a take-off from your carrier and ends with a landing.
One player, or two taking turns.

![A stage in progress: two enemy planes going down in flames.](shot:1942-play){full}

## Starting 1942

1942 has a disk of its own. Open that disk and double-click **1942.O88**.
Beside it is [[README.TXT]], the notes on the game.

The game needs a **VGA** or a **colour CGA** display. The window opens
with the title and a choice of one or two players. On a Hercules or other
black-and-white display you can see the title and the controls, but the
game itself will not start, and the window says **Fullscreen requires a VGA
or CGA display.**

![The launcher window, ready to take off.](shot:1942-launcher){full}

| Key | What it does |
|---|---|
| `1` or `Up` | One player |
| `2` or `Down` | Two players |
| `Enter`, `Space` or `F` | Take off |
| `H` | Show the controls, and hide them again |

You can also click **1 PLAYER** or **2 PLAYERS** to start straight away.

The game fills the screen. Loading the pictures for your display takes a
moment the first time.

## Flying

| Key | What it does |
|---|---|
| Arrow keys | Fly in any direction |
| `Space` or `Z` | Fire. Hold the key down to keep firing |
| `X` | Roll. You loop out of harm's way and cannot be hit. Three rolls a stage |
| `P` | Pause, and press again to carry on |
| `M` | Sound off and on |
| `C` | On a CGA display, change the colours |
| `N` | Start a new game |
| `Esc` or `F` | Go back to the desktop |
| `Enter` | Carry on from the mission results |

![The controls page in the launcher window.](shot:1942-help){right}

The line at the top of the screen shows your score, **L** for the lives you
have left, **R** for the rolls left in this stage, and the stage. Stages
count down, as in the arcade game: you start at **STAGE 32** and the
mission is complete after stage 01.


Enemy fighters dive, loop and sweep across the screen, and only shoot when
they are close and pointing at you. Bigger planes take several hits to bring down. A
giant bomber fills the background on stages 7, 15, 23 and 31.

## Power-ups

Shoot down a whole orange formation, including any groups that follow it,
and it leaves a **POW** behind. Fly into it to collect one of these:

- stronger guns, which fire a wider spread
- a blast that clears the screen of enemies
- two wingmen who fly beside you and fire with you. Enemy fire or a
  collision can knock them out
- an extra roll
- an extra life

A secret bonus plane appears once you have shot down enough enemies.

## Scoring and lives

You earn extra lives at 20,000 and 80,000 points, and then every 80,000
points after that. After each landing the **MISSION RESULTS** screen shows
the share of enemies you downed and gives a bonus for it, and another for
each roll you did not use. Press `Enter` to go on.

The high score is kept until you close 1942. It is not saved to disk.

## Two players

Choose **2 PLAYERS** before take-off. The players take turns: when one
loses a life, the other flies. Each keeps a separate score, lives, weapons
and stage, and a player coming back restarts the stage they were on.

## Pausing and going back to the desktop

`P` freezes the game, with **PAUSED** and **P TO RESUME** on the screen.

![The game paused.](shot:1942-paused){full}

![Back on the desktop, with the game ready to resume.](shot:1942-resume){left}

`Esc` or `F` puts you back on the desktop with the game kept where it was.
The launcher then offers **RESUME GAME** and **NEW GAME**. `Enter` picks up
exactly where you left off, and `N` or a click on **NEW GAME** goes back to
choosing players.


## Sound

With an AdLib or Sound Blaster card, 1942 plays FM music and sound effects.
A Sound Blaster (DSP version 2 or later) also plays recorded gunfire and
explosions. Without a card you hear simple tones on the PC speaker. `M`
turns everything off and on, and `P` pauses the music. The sound stops when
you go back to the desktop.

## Good to know

- On VGA the playfield is in the middle of a 320 by 240 screen. On CGA it
  uses the 320 by 200 four-colour screen, and `C` cycles three colour sets:
  Ocean, Jungle and Night.
- The game never runs faster than the PC's own clock, so a fast machine
  plays at the same pace as an XT. On a slow XT a busy screen can slow the
  game down a little.
- 1942 is written for a machine with 640 KB of memory. It holds 61 KB while
  it sits on the desktop, and the pictures for the game add about 18 KB on
  VGA and 53 KB on CGA. If there is not enough, the launcher says **Not
  enough memory for the 1942 graphics.**
- Everything the game needs is inside 1942.O88. Copy that one file and you
  have the whole game.
