# Times

Times is a light book serif with the proportions of Times New Roman, the
classic newspaper and book face. It is the one light face on the system:
its strokes are a single pixel thick, so it looks fine and elegant on a VGA
or a Hercules screen. On a CGA, whose pixels are tall and narrow, it reads
thin; Charter and Noto Serif are the sturdier serifs for that screen.

![Font Viewer showing Times with a sample typed in.](shot:times-f88)

## Where it comes from

This face was fitted for os8088 onto the screen's grid from Tinos, by the
Tinos Project Authors, which has the same character widths as Times New
Roman. The `$` was redrawn by hand so that the top of its S is not cut off.
The face is named for the typeface it stands in for, Times, rather than the
font it was drawn from.

Tinos is under the SIL Open Font License 1.1, and so is this face.
`LICENSE.TXT` in the same folder carries the licence and the authors' credit.

## What is in it

One style of 95 characters: the capital and small letters, the figures and
the punctuation of plain ASCII. There are no accented letters. Capitals are
8 pixels tall in a 12-pixel line, with 2 pixels between lines. Letters are
4, 6 or 8 pixels wide.

## Using it

The face lives in `SYSTEM/FONTS` on the system disk. Double-click
**TIMES.F88** to look at it in Font Viewer.

Microsoft Word, the C edition of Word and Scribe list it in the **Font** box
on their ribbon as **Times**. Pick it and the whole document is shown in it;
the ribbon then names it in full, **Times Roman**.
