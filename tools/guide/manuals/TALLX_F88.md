# Tallx

Tallx is os8088's own 8 by 8 screen face, offered as a typeface you can
choose. Every character sits in the same small square, so it looks like
ordinary screen text, but with tall lowercase letters and short capitals in
the manner of the early Macintosh. Because each character is exactly the
size of the built-in screen letters, a document set in Tallx keeps its line
breaks and its length: only the shapes of the letters change.

![Font Viewer showing Tallx with a sample typed in.](shot:tallx-f88)

## Where it comes from

Tallx was drawn for os8088 by hand, from the proportions of the Macintosh's
system face rather than from any outline. The lowercase is 5 pixels tall
against 6 for the capitals, the dots are square and every stem is two pixels
thick. The capital `I` keeps its serifs so it is never mistaken for a
lowercase `l`, and the figure zero is narrower than the capital `O`. It is
covered by the same MIT licence as the rest of os8088.

The system's menus and windows use the letters built into your PC itself,
not Tallx. Tallx is a face you pick for a document.

## What is in it

One style of 95 characters: the capital and small letters, the figures and
the punctuation of plain ASCII. There are no accented letters. Every
character is 8 pixels wide and 8 tall, with no space between lines.

## Using it

The face lives in `SYSTEM/FONTS` on the system disk. Double-click
**TALLX.F88** to look at it in Font Viewer.

Microsoft Word, the C edition of Word and Scribe list it in the **Font** box
on their ribbon as **Tallx**. Pick it and the whole document is shown in it,
laid out exactly as it was in **Pica**, the built-in face.
