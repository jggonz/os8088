# Noto Serif

Noto Serif is a bold, condensed serif typeface: sturdy letters with firm
serifs, drawn narrow by their designer. It is the face to read a document in
on a black-and-white screen such as a Hercules or a CGA, where thinner
serifs break up.

![Font Viewer showing Noto Serif with a sample typed in.](shot:noto-f88)

## Where it comes from

This face was fitted for os8088 onto the screen's grid from Noto Serif by
the Noto Project Authors, using the font's own condensed, bold drawing rather
than squeezing the regular one.

Noto Serif is under the SIL Open Font License 1.1, and so is this face.
`LICENSE.TXT` in the same folder carries the licence and the authors' credit.

## What is in it

One style of 95 characters: the capital and small letters, the figures and
the punctuation of plain ASCII. There are no accented letters. Capitals are
8 pixels tall in a 12-pixel line, with 2 pixels between lines. Letters are
4, 6 or 8 pixels wide.

## Using it

The face lives in `SYSTEM/FONTS` on the system disk. Double-click
**NOTO.F88** to look at it in Font Viewer.

Microsoft Word, the C edition of Word and Scribe list it in the **Font** box
on their ribbon as **Noto**. Pick it and the whole document is shown in it;
the ribbon then names it in full, **Noto Serif**.

Charter is the other sturdy serif. Times is the light one.
