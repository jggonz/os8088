# Desk Clock

Desk Clock is a clock that lives on the desktop itself, under every window.
It has nine faces, from a classic dial to a red LED display and a flip
clock, in three sizes. Under the face a panel shows the time, the date and a
daily alarm that rings with Snooze and Dismiss. It stays where you put it,
and once it is open it comes back by itself every time the machine starts.

![Desk Clock on the desktop with its settings window, the Red LED face previewed on the clock.](shot:deskclok-desktop){full}

## Starting Desk Clock

Desk Clock is on the system disk, in the `STIKAPPS` folder. Double-click
**DESKCLOK.O88** to open it. You can also pick **Desk Clock** from the
**Sticky Apps** menu on the desktop's menu bar (see below).

Only one Desk Clock can be open. If you try to open a second, the menu bar
says **Desk Clock is open**.

The first time, it opens near the left edge of the screen as a Classic dial,
Medium size, with the panel under it and a name tag, **Desk Clock**, below.
It has no title bar and no dock tile. Windows always cover it, never the
other way round.

## The clock

![The Classic face, Medium, with the seconds on.](shot:deskclok-clock){right}

Under a dial, the panel shows the time in large figures, then the date, such
as `WED 14 OCT 2026`. The last row has a bell and the alarm: **Alarm off**,
or the time it is set for, such as `Alarm 07:30`. The gear at the right of
that row opens the settings.

On a digital face the face shows the time itself, and the panel shows the
date and the alarm.

What you can click:

- The bell's row opens the **Alarm** window.
- The gear opens **Desk Clock Settings**. So does a double-click anywhere
  else on the clock.
- Anything else on the clock moves it: press and drag.

The clock follows the system's time. Set the date and time, and whether the
clock is 12- or 24-hour, in the Control Panel.

## The faces and sizes

![A Large Red LED face, with Clock face only on.](shot:deskclok-led){left}

The nine faces are **Classic**, **Memphis '80** and **Neon '90**, which are
dials with hands, and **Mono Digital**, **Casio LCD**, **Casio VGA**, **Red
LED**, **Amber LED** and **Flip Clock**, which show figures. On a
black-and-white screen each face is drawn in black, white and greys.

Each face comes in three sizes, **Small**, **Medium** and **Large**. Three
more settings change what the clock shows:

- **Clock face only** hides the panel and the name tag, so only the face is
  on the desktop. A ringing alarm still shows its Snooze and Dismiss row.
- **Show Seconds** adds a second hand to a dial, and the seconds to a
  digital face and to the panel.
- **24-Hour Clock** shows `14:05` rather than `2:05` with `PM`.

Change them from the menus, the keys or the settings window. A change made
from the menus or the keys is saved straight away.

## Desk Clock Settings

![The settings window, with Red LED selected.](shot:deskclok-settings){right}

Press `Enter`, choose **Clock > Settings...**, click the gear, or
double-click the clock. **Desk Clock Settings** opens beside the clock.

At the top are three check boxes, **Clock face only**, **Seconds** and
**24-hour**, and the **Size** button. Each press on the Size button moves
to the next size: Small, Medium, Large, then Small again.

Under **Choose a face** are the nine faces, each a small working clock
showing the time now. Click one to choose it; the line at the bottom says
which, such as **Selected: Red LED**.

Every change shows on the clock at once, so you can try faces and sizes on
the desktop.

- **Apply** keeps what you see and leaves the window open.
- **OK** keeps it and closes the window.
- **Cancel**, `Esc` or the close box put the clock back as it was at the
  last Apply and close the window.

Keys in the settings window:

| Key | What it does |
|---|---|
| Left, Right | The face before or after |
| Up, Down | The face a row above or below |
| `F`, `S`, `H` | Ticks or clears Clock face only, Seconds or 24-hour |
| `Z` | The next size |
| `A` | Apply |
| `Enter` | OK |
| `Esc` | Cancel |

## The alarm

![The Alarm window.](shot:deskclok-alarm){right}

Click the bell's row, press `A` or choose **Clock > Alarm...** to open the
**Alarm** window. It has:

- **Alarm on**, a check box that sets the alarm or turns it off.
- **Time**, the hour and the minute. On a 12-hour clock a third box shows
  `AM` or `PM`. The `-` and `+` buttons step the hour or the minute,
  whichever is selected.
- **Label**, up to 16 letters that the clock shows when it rings. It starts
  as `Wake up`.

**OK** (`Enter`) sets the alarm. **Cancel**, `Esc` or the close box leave it
as it was.

Keys in the Alarm window:

| Key | What it does |
|---|---|
| `Tab`, Left, Right | Move between the hour, the minute, AM/PM and the label |
| Up, Down, `+`, `-` | Step the hour or the minute |
| `0` to `9` | Type the hour or the minute |
| `A`, `P` | Switch AM and PM, on the AM/PM box |
| `Space` | Ticks or clears Alarm on (not in the label) |
| `Backspace` | Removes the last letter of the label |
| `Enter`, `Esc` | OK, Cancel |

Clicking the AM/PM box also switches it.

## When it rings

![The alarm ringing, with Snooze and Dismiss under it.](shot:deskclok-ringing){left}

The alarm rings once a day, at its time. The menu bar shows `Alarm:` and
the label, the speaker chirps for about 30 seconds, and the panel shows the
alarm's time and label with **Snooze** and **Dismiss** under them. They stay
there until you pick one. Nothing pops up and nothing stops you working.

- **Snooze** (or `Z`) rings it again in five minutes. The panel's alarm row
  says when, for example `Zz 07:35`.
- **Dismiss** (or `D`) puts it away until tomorrow. It also cancels a
  snooze.

While the alarm rings, `Esc` dismisses it too.

## The menu bar and the keyboard

![The Clock menu.](shot:deskclok-menu){right}

The clock's menus show while it has the menu bar. Click the clock to give it
the bar. Press `Esc` to hand the bar and the keyboard back to the window in
front.

The **Clock** menu:

| Item | Key | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| Settings... | `Enter` | Opens Desk Clock Settings |
| Alarm... | `A` | Opens the Alarm window |
| Clock Face Only | `F` | Hides or shows the panel and the name tag |
| Show Seconds | `S` | Shows or hides the seconds |
| 24-Hour Clock | `H` | Switches between a 24-hour and a 12-hour clock |
| Snooze | `Z` | Rings the alarm again in five minutes |
| Dismiss | `D` | Stops the alarm until tomorrow |

The three settings are marked `*` when they are on. **Snooze** and
**Dismiss** are greyed with **(not ringing)** when there is nothing to stop.

The **Face** menu lists the nine faces and the **Size** menu the three
sizes; the one in use is marked `*`.

Other keys:

| Key | What it does |
|---|---|
| Left, Right | The face before or after |
| Up, Down | A size larger or smaller |
| `Esc` | Dismisses a ringing alarm, or gives the menu bar back |

The **Desk Clock** menu at the left of the bar has **About Desk Clock**,
which shows a line about it on the menu bar, and **Close**.

## Moving and closing it

Point at the clock's name tag for a moment and it turns into a black handle
with a close box at the left and a grip at the right. With Clock face only
on there is no tag, but the handle still appears when you point just under
the face. Drag the clock by its tag, or by its face, to move it anywhere on
the desktop. It remembers the place.

To close it, click the close box on the handle, or choose **Close** from the
**Desk Clock** menu. Closing it is the only thing that stops it coming back.
If it is open when you restart or switch off, it opens again at the next
start, in the same place, with the same face and alarm.

## The Sticky Apps menu

Desk Clock is a Sticky App: a small program that belongs to the desktop.
When no window is in front, the desktop's menu bar has a **Sticky Apps**
menu ([[STIKMENU.DRV]]), where Desk Clock is listed as **Nine faces & an
alarm**. Pick it when it is marked `Add` to open it, or when it is ticked
`On` to close it.

## Files

The clock keeps its face, size, settings, alarm and place on the screen in
`SYSTEM\APPDATA\DESKCLOK.DAT` on the system disk. If the file is missing or
damaged, the clock starts as it does the first time: Classic, Medium, the
seconds off, the system's 12- or 24-hour choice, and the alarm off at 07:00
with the label `Wake up`.

## Good to know

- There is one alarm, and it rings every day at the same time while it is
  on.
- On a 4.77 MHz PC the seconds keep the machine a little busy. With them off
  the clock changes once a minute and costs almost nothing.
- The colours are fixed for each face and do not follow the desktop's
  theme.
- A machine without a clock card starts with the wrong time. Set the date
  and time in the Control Panel.
- On the small 128KB system, Desk Clock opens as an ordinary window with a
  title bar, and it does not come back by itself at the next start. There
  the seconds move every two seconds.
