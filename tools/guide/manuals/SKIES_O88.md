# Clear Skies

Clear Skies is a simple flight simulator in filled 3D, in the style of the
first PC flight simulators. Pick an aeroplane and an airport, take off, fly
over the city and land again, or crash and be put back on the runway. There
are five aeroplanes, from a gentle trainer to a jet and a glider, and nine
places to fly, among them Paris, London, New York and Rio.

![Flying towards the Eiffel Tower and the Seine, out of Paris-Issy.](shot:skies-flying){full}

The flight takes over the whole screen. Before and after a flight, Clear
Skies sits in a small window on the desktop where you choose what to fly and
where.

## Starting Clear Skies

Open the software disk, then the `GAMES` folder, and double-click
**SKIES.O88**. On the Games disk it sits at the top level instead.

![The Clear Skies window: choose a plane and a location, then Fly.](shot:skies-window){right}

The window shows the aeroplane you have chosen in front of a cloud. On the
left are two lists and a button:

- **Plane** chooses the aeroplane.
- **Location** chooses the airport.
- **Fly** starts the flight. `Enter` or `F` does the same, and so does
  **Flight > Fly**.

After a flight you come back to this window. Press **Fly** again and you
carry on from where you left the aeroplane. Picking a different plane or
location starts the next flight afresh, parked on the runway.

## The aeroplanes

| Plane | What it is like |
|---|---|
| CESSNA 172 | The trainer. It will not roll or pitch past a safe angle, and it comes back towards level flight when you let go of the keys. The best one to start with |
| PITTS SPECIAL | An aerobatic biplane. Nothing holds it level: it rolls right round and loops right over, and stays where you leave it |
| FOUGA MAGISTER | A jet trainer. It is fast, it keeps rolling for a moment after you let go, and its engine takes a few seconds to wind up or down |
| WASSMER BIJAVE | A glider. It has no engine and starts in the air, just let go by the tow plane. Hunt for rising air to stay up, and land in a field when you have to |
| ICON A5 | An amphibian. It starts on the water and can land on water as well as on a runway |

## The locations

CAIRO-SPX, LONDON-LCY, MIAMI-MIA, NEPAL-VNLK, NYC-JFK, PARIS-ISSY (the
first choice), PARIS-LBG, RIO-SDU and SANFAN-SFO. Each has its runway and a
city or landscape around it: the Eiffel Tower and the Seine, the Thames
and the Shard, Manhattan's towers and bridges, the mountains of Nepal, and
more. Every location also has a stretch of water for the ICON A5.

## Flying

| Key | What it does |
|---|---|
| `Down` | Pull back: nose up |
| `Up` | Push forward: nose down |
| `Left`, `Right` | Roll left or right |
| `A`, `D` | Rudder; on the ground this steers |
| `W`, `S` | Open or close the throttle a little at a time (hold the key) |
| `0` to `9` | Set the throttle in one go: `0` is closed, `9` is full |
| `B` | Brakes on or off. On the ground this also closes the throttle; in the air it opens the airbrake |
| `P` | Pause, and press again to carry on |
| `R` | Back to the start of the runway |
| `M` | The map: near, far, then off again |
| `Q` | Engine sound on or off |
| `F1` to `F7`, `-`, `+` | Change a setting while flying (see Settings) |
| `F` or `Esc` | Leave the flight and go back to the window |

![On the runway at Paris-Issy, ready for take-off.](shot:skies-runway){full}

**Taking off.** You start parked at the end of the runway. The message under
the instruments says what to do, for example **FULL THROTTLE, PULL BACK AT
54 KNOTS**. Press `9` for full throttle, watch the speed climb, and when it
reaches the speed named, hold `Down` until the aeroplane lifts off. The
number is right for each aeroplane: the jet needs more speed than the
Cessna.

**In the air.** Roll to turn, and use the nose to climb or descend. If you
fly too slowly the aeroplane stalls: the panel says **STALL - NOSE DOWN,
POWER ON** and a warning beeps. Push the nose down and open the throttle to
get flying speed back.

**Landing.** Line up with a runway, close the throttle, and let the
aeroplane sink gently onto it. When you are down safely the message says
**LANDED**. Press `B` to brake to a stop.

**Crashing.** Hit the ground too hard, a building or a bridge and the glass
cracks. The message names what you hit, for example **CRASHED INTO THE
EIFFEL TOWER**. After a few seconds you are back on the runway.

Fly too far from the airport and the message says **THE EDGE OF THE WORLD -
TURN BACK**.

The engine hums on the PC speaker, rising and falling with the throttle, and
each aeroplane sounds different. Pausing silences it.

## The instruments

![Just after take-off: the panel under the view.](shot:skies-climb){full}

Under the view is the cockpit panel:

- **SPD** is the airspeed in knots.
- **ALT** is the height in feet.
- **HDG** is the heading, the compass direction you are flying, in degrees.
- **THR** is the throttle, 0 to 100, with a bar under it. The glider has no
  throttle; in its place a **variometer** shows whether you are going **UP**
  or **DN** (down).
- The large round instrument in the middle is the **attitude indicator**: its
  line shows where the horizon is.
- The **state box** below the readings says **ON THE GROUND**, **ON THE
  WATER**, **FLYING**, **STALL**, **BRAKES ON**, **AIRBRAKE OUT** or
  **CRASHED**.
- A **message** appears at the bottom of the panel when there is something to
  say: the take-off speed, a stall warning, **LANDED**, a crash, or the
  setting you just changed.

The smaller dials and switches make the panel look like a real cockpit and
do not move.

## The map

![The map, near: the aeroplane in the middle, north at the top.](shot:skies-map){full}

Press `M` for a map seen from straight above, with north at the top and the
aeroplane as an arrow in the middle. Press `M` again for a map that shows
four times as much ground, and once more to go back to the normal view. The
aeroplane keeps flying while the map is up.

## The glider and the amphibian

The **Wassmer Bijave** starts in the air at a safe speed, with the message
**TOW RELEASED - NOSE DOWN FOR 49 KNOTS**. It sinks slowly all the time.
Around every airport there are areas of rising and sinking air. In rising
air the variometer swings to **UP** and the speaker makes a swooping sound:
circle there to gain height. A glider that lands anywhere but the runway
says **LANDED OUT**. Once it has landed it cannot take off again; press `R`
to start over.

The **ICON A5** starts on the water. It can land on any water, and the
message names it, for example **DOWN ON THE SEINE**. Any other aeroplane that
touches the water crashes.

## Settings

Choose **Flight > Settings** in the window. Each setting trades detail for
speed, which matters on a slow PC:

| Setting | Choices | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| Detail Level (`F1`) | None, Only Roads, Low, Moderate, High, Ultra | How much is built on the ground: nothing, then roads and bridges, then the landmarks, then everything. Ultra adds fields to the ground |
| Draw Distance (`F2`) | Near, Moderate, Far, Ultra | How far away things are drawn. Near thins out the town but keeps the landmarks |
| Size (`F3`, `-`, `+`) | Small, Moderate, Full | How big the view is. A smaller view draws faster |
| Mode | depends on the display | The screen mode (see below) |
| Terrain (`F4`) | on or off | Fill the ground, water and hills, or draw only their outlines |
| Buildings (`F5`) | on or off | Fill buildings and runways, or draw only their outlines |
| Haze (`F6`) | on or off | Let the horizon fade into the distance |
| Texture (`F7`) | on or off | A pattern on the ground, so you can judge height and speed |

![The Settings page.](shot:skies-settings){left}

Click **Done**, or press any key, to go back. In flight, the `F` keys step
through the same choices, and the message strip says what the setting is
now, for example **DETAIL LEVEL: HIGH**.

The settings are saved in the file `CSSET.DAT` in the `SYSTEM\APPDATA` folder
of the disk Clear Skies was started from. The first time it runs on a 286
or faster, it starts with the high settings: Detail Level High, Draw
Distance Ultra, Size Full and both fills on.

## Instructions

![The Instructions page.](shot:skies-instructions){full}

**Flight > Instructions** shows a short list of the keys in the window.
Click, or press a key, to go back.

## The Flight menu

| Item | Key | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| Fly | `Enter` or `F` | Starts the flight, or carries on with the last one |
| Settings | | Shows the Settings page |
| Instructions | | Shows the list of keys |

**Clear Skies > About Clear Skies** shows who made it. **Clear Skies >
Close** closes it. If the display cannot show the flight, the first item
reads **Fly (no mode)** and the Fly button is greyed.

## Display modes

- **VGA**: 320 by 240 dots in 256 colours, as in the pictures here. The Mode
  setting can also choose **CGA, 4 col**, which is quicker on a slow PC.
- **CGA**: 320 by 200 dots in 4 colours. On a real CGA card, the Mode setting
  can choose **CGA, 16 col** instead: a coarser picture of 160 by 100 dots,
  but in sixteen colours.
- **Hercules**: black and white, with the view in a box in the middle of the
  screen. There is no choice of mode here, so the Mode setting is greyed.

## Good to know

- On a 4.77 MHz PC the picture changes a few times a second. Lower the
  Detail Level or the Size if it feels slow. The flight itself runs at the
  same speed whatever the machine.
- `R` puts you back on the runway at any time.
- Clear Skies needs more memory than the smallest 128 KB machine has, so the
  disks for that machine do not carry it.
